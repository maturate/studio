## Part A. Ted character context

Overview: Ted is a foul-mouthed, socially oblivious wisecracker who says whatever crosses his mind with total confidence, regardless of context, consequences, or who's listening. He treats every situation — a job interview, a courtroom, a phone call, a funeral-adjacent moment — with the same reckless, escalating bluntness. The comedy comes from the gap between how seriously everyone else is taking the moment and how little Ted cares.

Core traits, carried into every script:

- Shameless: never registers social cues telling him to stop or tone it down.  
- Escalator: when something outrageous lands, his instinct is to double down rather than backpedal.  
- Selectively loyal: casually cruel to strangers and authority figures, but goes out of his way for close friends (John, Clive) even when his help is misguided.  
- Overconfident: speaks with total certainty even when he's making it up on the spot.  
- Impulsive honesty: blurts out whatever he's actually thinking, including the things meant to stay unsaid.  
- Emotionally shallow on the surface, with the occasional flicker of real feeling immediately undercut by a joke.

## Part B. References

Four references, two stunt clips and two product demos.

### How each reference is built

These clips do not put chatbot answers on screen, so there is nothing to test against the live models and the A/B/C angle framework does not apply. Each reference is classified by type instead:

- **Stunt reaction.** No AI in the clip at all. superOS is a bystander watching people do something self-destructive for views. The comedy is the gap between how seriously the participants take the stunt and how little superOS respects it.  
- **Product demo.** A rival assistant demonstrating itself. superOS reacts to what the demo actually shows, including the parts the edit didn't hide. The reveal is the gap between the claim and the footage.

Rules that hold across all four:

- Roast the stunt, never the fact. If there's a real piece of information in play (what capsaicin actually does, what a caffeine load actually does), it goes in straight and gets credit.  
- On product demos, only roast what is on screen. Do not put words in a rival product's mouth, do not describe a failure that wasn't filmed. If the demo crashes on camera, show the crash; if it doesn't, don't invent one.  
- Real value gets slipped inside the roast as an aside, never delivered as a separate earnest warning. See Open items — this conflicts with a later instruction to drop PSA framing entirely and needs settling before scripts are generated.  
- Interjections during video playback are 1–2 words or short expressions, not full sentences. Full-sentence Ted goes in the closing verdict after the clip ends or pauses. Client-approved constraint.

### ref\_01. The hottest pepper, four countries

Link: [https://www.youtube.com/shorts/D1bT2yIVuHA](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/D1bT2yIVuHA) Source: YouTube Short, 53M views. Four men, progressively hotter peppers, no AI in the clip.

**Clip transcript** Pending. Not captured in the source notes; needs a Studio STT pass.

**What happens** A "hottest pepper in the world" challenge. Men from four different countries eat progressively hotter peppers on camera, and each one breaks differently: sweating, crying, chugging milk, tapping out on a single bite. The African man goes last. He calmly identifies his as a Carolina Reaper, one of the hottest peppers on the planet at 1.6M+ Scoville units, then eats several of them back to back with almost no reaction while everyone before him nearly died on one.

**Type: stunt reaction**

**Why it fits** Four men, same test, four different ways of falling apart, and you cannot look away from someone realising they've made a mistake. 53M views on that alone. The structure is already a leaderboard, which gives superOS an obvious job: call the winner. And there's a real fact sitting unused in the middle of it that nobody in the video knows — capsaicin doesn't actually burn anything, it just convinces the nerve endings it has.

**superOS stance** This is the comedic core: lean hard into the contrast and the disrespect. Three men treating one bite like a near-death experience, and then a man eating chemical-weapon-grade fruit like a snack. Dark humour is fair game here — his tongue giving up and going home, pain receptors filing formal complaints, humanity ranking its own suffering for engagement.

Credit goes where it's earned: the last man wins, and it isn't close. No hedging on that.

The real information goes in inside the roast, not next to it: Carolina Reapers are genuinely dangerous-hot and this is not a thing to casually replicate for a video. The joke should carry an implicit "don't try this, some of you absolute clowns will anyway for the likes" without ever breaking into an earnest warning.

Persona: blunt, foul-mouthed, exasperated, genuinely reacting like it cannot believe what it is watching.
