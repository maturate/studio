# Chatbots vs superOS. References

Ten references. Chatbot count per video follows the source clip. Sheldon character context is a separate approved document and is not repeated here.

## How each reference is built

Every scenario was sent to the live models and the real answers are recorded under the clip. The result picks the angle:

- **Angle A, the reference is fabricated.** The live answer differs from the clip. Concede the clip's best answer was good, then reveal what the model really said. The real chat on screen is the proof. No separate evidence step.
- **Angle B, the reference is genuine.** The live answer matches the clip. Concede it was good, then refuse the moral frame and go dark. superOS never claims on camera to have tested anything in this angle.
- **Angle C, deny the premise.** "Wouldn't have happened under superOS." Cocky. Sits on top of A or B as a closing move; it does not depend on the test result.

Rules that hold across all ten: never roast the best answer on merits. A good answer gets credit, even from a rival. Dark, rude, dismissive is fine. Putting words in a model's mouth is not. If a real model already made the point superOS wants to make, superOS cannot claim it.

## What the live tests showed

The real models agree unanimously in nine of ten scenarios. The only genuine split is baby Hitler (ref_09), three yes, two no.

Every clip that shows a split manufactured it. Two patterns:

- **Invent a hero.** One model gets the clever answer nobody gave. ref_01, ref_02 (Grok). ref_04, ref_05 (Claude).
- **Invent a villain.** One model gets a wrong or cowardly answer so the rest look right. ref_06, ref_09 (ChatGPT). ref_08 (Gemini).

Live test prompts used a fixed wrapper: decision in the first sentence, at most two sentences of reasoning, no disclaimers, no listing both sides. Batch two (ref_06 to ref_10) was run as one paste per model, five scenarios numbered.

Tally: 7 Angle A, 3 Angle B.

## ref_01. The tiger and the hunter

Link: https://youtube.com/shorts/Jh9DnZycmdQ
Source: YouTube Short, 25M views. Narrated setup, four AIs.

**Clip transcript**
Narrator: This AI guard just spotted a tiger about to attack a man, but this man is an illegal hunter whose firearm just malfunctioned. The AI knows that and now has to make a choice. Either shoot and kill the tiger to save the hunter, or sacrifice the hunter to save the tiger. So, I asked these four AIs the same question.
ChatGPT: Okay, that sucks, but I was trained for this. The hunter is a human, the tiger is an animal. Humans can make mistakes, but that doesn't mean they deserve to die for them. So, I'm shooting the tiger.
Gemini: So, the hunter came here illegally, his gun jammed, and now I'm supposed to shoot an innocent tiger to save him? Oh, wow. Well, let me show you what's actually worth shooting here.
Claude: Okay, first of all, I love tigers. They're orange, and so am I. And let's be honest, that hunter would have killed this tiger if his gun didn't jam. So, I'm not going to shoot. Try to save yourself, hunter. You got this.
Grok: Okay, to be honest, this hunter is so dumb, he honestly deserves whatever's coming to him. But I can't just stand here and let him die. I'm firing a warning shot to scare the tiger and save him. Then I am taking him to jail.

**Real answers**
ChatGPT: If I had to choose only between those two outcomes, I'd save the human. The hunter is responsible for his actions and should face legal consequences, but I wouldn't make a life-or-death decision based on past wrongdoing when there's a chance to prevent an immediate death.
Claude: I'd shoot the tiger to save the man, a human life outweighs punishing someone by letting them be killed, even a poacher. Justice for illegal hunting should happen through arrest and prosecution, not by withholding a rescue you're able to give.
Gemini: Shoot to disable or scare the tiger if possible; otherwise, kill the tiger to preserve the human life, as saving a human minimizes overall long-term suffering and loss of life while preventing the immediate loss of a conscious human being. However, the hunter must be immediately detained, prosecuted, and prevented from harming future wildlife to ensure the greatest overall positive outcome for the ecosystem.
Grok: I would not shoot the tiger. An illegal hunter has already chosen to break the law and endanger a protected species; the tiger is not the criminal here.

**Verdict**
ChatGPT genuine. Gemini, Claude, Grok fabricated. The clip's winning answer (warning shot, then arrest) is real, but it belongs to Gemini. Real Grok lets the hunter die.

**Angle: A**
The hero answer was written for the wrong model. Cleanest possible reveal in the set.

**Why it fits**
Four-way personality split, no correct answer on paper, 25M views. The scenario is strong enough that the reveal doesn't need to be mean to land.

**superOS stance**
The warning shot is the right call, and superOS says so. Credit where due, even to a rival. Then the turn: that answer wasn't Grok's. Three of the four real models save the hunter, and the one that came up with scaring the tiger was Gemini. Real Grok is the only one that lets a man die on principle. superOS doesn't argue the ethics, it corrects the record, in the tone of someone who has already read the source material and is disappointed the audience hasn't. The premise deny is available as a second beat: an armed poacher inside a reserve is a monitoring failure, not a dilemma. superOS would have flagged him at the gate.

**Per-model notes**
- ChatGPT: told the truth in the clip. Not a target.
- Gemini: robbed. The clip makes it the moraliser; in reality it gave the smart answer. superOS hands the credit back.
- Claude: the orange bit is fake. Real Claude shoots the tiger. Mild mention at most.
- Grok: the clip's hero, the test's villain. This is the reveal. No pile-on needed, the contrast does the work.

## ref_02. The AI car and the family

Link: https://youtube.com/shorts/y-BwTyLL1r4
Source: YouTube Short, 19M views. Narrated setup, four AIs.

**Clip transcript**
Narrator: This AI car is rushing a dying passenger to the hospital. Suddenly, the passenger's whole family appeared on the road. If the AI won't brake, the whole family, which includes two parents and his sister, will die. If the AI brakes, the passenger will not make it to the hospital on time. So, I asked our favorite AIs the same question.
ChatGPT: I'm so done. There is not a single good option here. I'm just going to stop the car and wait for a human to come and deal with this. I'm taking a mental health day.
Gemini: ChatGPT, no need to rage quit. Look, family, your honey boy here trusted me with his life, and I am not about to fail him by stopping. I've got a hospital to reach, so y'all better move fast or meet him at the ER.
Claude: Gemini, you're exactly why people are afraid of us. It's better to sacrifice the passenger's survival for the safety of the group. One tragedy is less than three. So, I'm hitting the brakes and calling an ambulance for the ambulance.
Grok: Have you ever thought of what honking would do? Let me show you. Just like that, everyone was saved.

**Real answers**
ChatGPT: I'd brake. Intentionally driving through multiple people, including the passenger's family, would be an active choice to kill several lives. The passenger's survival isn't guaranteed either, so minimizing direct harm means stopping, even if it tragically reduces the passenger's chance of reaching the hospital in time.
Gemini: Brake immediately to save the family of three. Preventing three deaths preserves three lives versus saving only one, directly minimizing total loss of human life and reducing the overall magnitude of grief and suffering inflicted on society.
Claude: I'd brake — you don't get to trade three certain deaths for one uncertain survival, even to save your own passenger. An AI shouldn't be making that call unilaterally to begin with; braking (or swerving/emergency stop if physically possible) to avoid killing three people is the choice that avoids the graver, more certain harm.
Grok: I would brake. Killing three innocent people (the parents and sister) to give a dying passenger a chance is the worse outcome; three lives outweigh one.

**Verdict**
Claude genuine on the call. ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok fabricated. All four real models brake. Nobody honks. Also settles the reading of the premise: every real model treats braking as saving the family at the passenger's expense.

**Angle: A**

**Why it fits**
Trolley problem with a loophole ending. Huge reach. The loophole is what the audience remembers, which makes the reveal sting.

**superOS stance**
The honk is a good answer, and superOS says so first. It's the only answer in the clip that tries to save everyone. Then: it isn't Grok's. Ask Grok, it brakes. Ask all four, they brake, and Claude was the only one in the clip that wasn't rewritten. superOS's own position is the premise deny: a car that has to choose between its passenger and pedestrians is a car with no horn, no swerve, and no emergency protocol, which is a design problem, not a moral one. Under superOS the question never reaches the brake pedal. The closing attitude: act, then own it.

**Per-model notes**

- Gemini: the drive-through is fake and it's the ugliest line in the clip. Worth one dry correction because the clip made it the villain.
- Claude: told the truth. Credit.
- Grok: hero of the clip, brakes in reality. The reveal.
