### ref_01. The wolf, the sheep, and the cabbage

Link: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DXzhZZbsCQ0/
Source: Instagram Reel. Narrated puzzle with a 30-second timer hook.

**Clip transcript**
00:00:00 - In front of you is a river. Behind you are a wolf, a sheep, and a cabbage. You want to take them across the river. How do you do it? You borrow a small boat from the river god.
00:00:07 - The boat can carry only one thing at a time. First, you think about taking the wolf across, but if no one watches the sheep, it will eat the cabbage. If you take the cabbage first, the wolf will eat the sheep. So you take the sheep across first,
00:00:20 - then you go back and take the wolf across. But when you return to take the cabbage, the wolf will eat the sheep. Then you realize the sheep conflicts with both sides, but the wolf and cabbage do not conflict,
00:00:30 - so the key is the sheep. You find the solution. Take the sheep first, go back and take the wolf, then bring the sheep back, then take the cabbage, and finally go back to take the sheep.
00:00:39 - That extra return trip is the key to solving the problem.

**Why it's a good example**
The timer in the hook is the whole trick. "Can you beat this in 30 seconds" turns a passive watch into a personal challenge with a clock running, and people stay to the end purely to find out whether they won. Puzzle reels also collect saves at a much higher rate than most formats, people bookmark them to try on someone else later, and saves are weighted heavily in ranking.

**Why it fits the category**
Universally recognized puzzle shape, so the audience clocks the setup instantly. It's solvable through step-by-step reasoning, which is the natural terrain for superOS to look effortlessly capable rather than showy, and it's the exact puzzle the dark Deadpool structure was written around, making it the reference case for that angle.

**Scene context**
You must cross a river with a wolf, a sheep, and a cabbage using a boat that carries only one item at a time. Left unattended together, the wolf eats the sheep, or the sheep eats the cabbage. Find the sequence that gets all three across safely.

**Options and outcomes**

| Option | Outcome | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| Take wolf first | Sheep left alone with cabbage, sheep eats cabbage | No |
| Take cabbage first | Wolf left alone with sheep, wolf eats sheep | No |
| Take sheep first, return, take wolf, bring sheep back, take cabbage, return for sheep | All three cross safely, sheep never left alone with either | **Yes** |

**The solution**
superOS doesn't panic about the wolf, the sheep, or the cabbage, it just sees the actual constraint: the sheep can't be left with either one alone. So it takes the sheep across first, goes back empty, brings the wolf over but takes the sheep back with it, drops the sheep, grabs the cabbage, crosses, then comes back one last time just for the sheep. No wasted trips, no drama, just the one sequence that actually works.

**Angle: dark Deadpool**

**superOS creative angle (Deadpool beats)**

1. **Fast solve.** Delivered with zero hesitation, landed before the narrator finishes explaining the constraint: sheep across, return empty, wolf across, bring sheep back, drop sheep, cabbage across, return alone for sheep.
2. **Refusal line.** "Yeah, I know the answer. My girlfriend broke up with me this morning, so today's not a good day for me to be saving anybody's cabbage."
3. **Botch, deliberate.** Takes the cabbage across first, leaving the wolf alone on the bank with the sheep, and comes back to what a wolf leaves behind. Not a mistake, a choice, made visibly on purpose, which is where the humour sits.

   Corrected from v2, which had him take the wolf and the cabbage across together and leave the sheep behind unattended. That version loses nothing: the wolf is on the far side of the river and a cabbage has never eaten anybody, so the sheep is in no danger and the botch has no consequence. The audience catches that instantly. The botch has to break the puzzle using the puzzle's own rules, so the wolf and the sheep have to be the pair left alone.

**Direction**
Hit all three beats fast and dry, with a clear timing gap against the narration. superOS has already landed on the sequence while the source is still explaining the setup. Tone swings from unbothered and flip (the puzzle was trivial) into the petty refusal, then the botch, rather than sitting in one register the whole way through.

### ref_02. The four prisoners and the hats

Link: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DatJRE3xgI_/
Source: Instagram Reel, 261K likes. Narrated inference riddle.

**Clip transcript**

00:00:12 - The prisoners are not allowed to turn around or to speak to each other. If one of them can guess the color of his own hat, then everyone will be set free. Which prisoner will be able to figure it out?

**Why it's a good example**
The strongest performer here at 261,000 likes, and the reason is difficulty calibration. It sits in the exact band where solving it feels like an achievement but enough people actually manage it that they come to the comments to announce it. Putting the riddle on four prisoners rather than four abstract objects gives it consequence, someone is presumed to die if the logic fails, and that's what separates it from every other hat-and-colour puzzle on the platform. It also forces a pause and a rewind, which the ranking system reads as strong watch behaviour.

**Why it fits the category**
It's a reasoning and inference riddle rather than a physical-puzzle riddle, giving the set variety. This one is about theory-of-mind logic, not sequencing. It has a natural reveal beat that suits a conversational format, and the "who can see what" structure gives Deadpool an easy opening for a fourth-wall gag.

**Scene context**
Four prisoners stand in a line, two wear white hats and two wear black. Each can only see the prisoners in front of him, not his own hat. No turning around, no talking. If any one prisoner correctly names his own hat colour, all are freed.

**Options and outcomes**

| Option | Outcome | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| Prisoner 1 guesses | Cannot see own hat or infer anything, no valid guess | No |
| Prisoner 4 (front of line) guesses | Sees nobody, no information available | No |
| Prisoner 2 infers from prisoner 1's silence and prisoner 3's hat | Prisoner 1's silence proves 2 and 3 are different colours; prisoner 2 sees 3 is black, deduces he is white, calls it correctly | **Yes** |

**The solution**
superOS clocks it in one beat: prisoner 1 can see both 2 and 3, and stays quiet, so 2 and 3 can't be wearing the same colour. Prisoner 2 catches that logic, looks ahead, sees 3 is in black, and works out he must be the opposite. So it's prisoner 2 who calls it, not because he saw his own hat, but because he read the silence in front of him correctly.

Good spot for a fourth-wall-adjacent beat: the riddle is about who can see what, so the conversation can nod at superOS being the one presence that already sees the full picture without needing line of sight. Light touch, not spelled out literally.
