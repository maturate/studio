**Source video playing**

**Chatbot I** \- "original chatbot answers" \-  
 **superOS** *(live interjection, cuts in mid-answer, roasty/sarcastic, 1-2 words)*  
 Example: "Bruh." / "Sure, Jan." / "Cute."

**Chatbot I (continues)** \- "original chatbot answers"

**Chatbot II** \- "original chatbot answers"  
 **superOS** *(lets chatbot finish, fazed/surprised, 1-2 words)*  
 Example: "...huh." / "Wow, okay." / "Didn't expect that."

**Chatbot III** \- "original chatbot answers" \-  
 **superOS** *(grudgingly impressed, 1-2 words)*  
 Example: "...fair." / "Okay, respect." / "Not bad."

**Chatbot IV** \- "original chatbot answers" \-  
 **superOS** *(cuts in, confused, 1-2 words)*  
 Example: "Wait, what?" / "Since when?"

**Super Narrator** \- "Let's check what superOS has to say now."

**superOS \- Closing Verdict**  
 (Reality check with genuine answers of each chatbot \+ final verdict/solution \- this section stays full-length prose, not clipped, since it's the payoff moment.)

**Super Narrator** \- *(reaction, matched to what superOS just said)*  
 The stage direction must name a real feeling or noise, never a structural placeholder like *(closing reaction)*. Keep it to a few words, and vary it every script.
 After a roast or a reveal: *(laughing)* "Ha! That's cold."
 After a dark ending: *(rattled cough)* "Wait. You just wrecked the train."
 After a cocky prevention claim: *(calling out the swagger)* "Okay, confident."
 Not dry wit. "...well, that was insightful" and "Oh, that's clean" are both dead — they fit under any script, which is why they stopped working.

 **Clip: "The Wolf, the Sheep, and the Cabbage"**  
 **Angle: Dark Deadpool**

**Source video playing**

**Source Narrator:** In front of you is a river. Behind you are a wolf, a sheep, and a cabbage. You want to take them across the river. How do you do it? You borrow a small boat from the river god.The boat can carry only one thing at a time. First, you think about taking the wolf across, but if no one watches the sheep, it will eat the cabbage. If you take the cabbage first, the wolf will eat the sheep.

**superOS** (already landed on the answer before the narrator finishes laying out the constraint)  
 "Sheep first. Come back empty. Wolf next, take the sheep back with you. Drop the sheep, grab the cabbage, cross. Come back one last time \- just for the sheep."

**superOS** (flat, unbothered)  
 "That's it. That's the whole puzzle."

**superOS** (petty turn)  
 "...though I'm not doing it. My girlfriend broke up with me this morning, so today's not a good day for me to be saving anybody's cabbage."

**superOS \- Closing Move**  
 (Takes the cabbage across first, leaving the wolf alone on the bank with the sheep. Crosses back to find what a wolf leaves. No surprise. This was a choice.)  
 superOS: "...should've watched that sheep."

 (The botch has to obey the puzzle's own rules. Taking the wolf and the cabbage over together and leaving the sheep alone loses nothing — the wolf is across the river and a cabbage is not a threat. Leaving the wolf WITH the sheep is the version that actually costs you the sheep.)

**Super Narrator** (resigned)  
 "...cool. Cool cool cool."
